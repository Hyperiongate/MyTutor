## The class: a praise that teaches, then a walk-back that teaches it again

All five generator findings were on one op, `dst`, and all five said the same thing — the
walk-back repeats the praise. Reading the engine says it is neither a `dst` bug nor an
artefact of the sweep's transcript: **`_correct_beats` (build `sp`) plays the praise and
then the step-by-step to every child who answers correctly.** That is right when the praise
credits the answer and the walk-back teaches the method. It is wrong when the praise already
taught — then the child is told the same thing twice, in one breath.

Measured across the canon: the praise carries **8 or more content words**, the walk-back's
content is **70% or more already in the praise**, and the walk-back adds **3 or fewer ideas
of its own** (numbers are not ideas). That stood at **33 beats over 8 ops in 4 courses**:

| course | ops |
|---|---|
| Pre-Algebra | dst, tba, parf, uic, mlx |
| Algebra I | fnot |
| Pre-Calc | pyid, arsn |

All eight are credit lines now — the answer, then the ONE reason — with the method left to
the walk-back. `pyid` was the worst at 94%: the walk-back said the praise back almost word
for word, adding only "hundredths".

This is `xh`'s medicine one threshold lower. Every one of these praises sat **just under**
the 26-word line `xh` drew, so they survived the credit-line sweep while still doing the
teaching.

**Nothing was dropped.** Every idea the old praises carried is still spoken in the walk-back
— which is also the branch a child who MISSED hears, and therefore the one that needs it
most. PART 3nf checks that by text, not just that the praises got shorter: `arsn`'s "150
shares its sine with 30", `fnot`'s "nothing is timesed", `mlx`'s "copies of it, not beside
it", `uic`'s "fifths".

### Why the existing pin missed it

`wz` already pins "no praise is its walk-back in other words" — at 0.6 Jaccard **over the
union**, requiring the same numbers in the same order. This class is **directional**. A short
praise inside a long walk-back is correct and must stay legal: Entry's "That's it! 2 plus 3
equals 5." before a walk-back that counts the stars is exactly right. Coverage of the
walk-back BY the praise separates the two; the union does not.

### The one allowed exception, named

Basic's `vol`. Its praise is a bare credit line already ("3 times 2 times 1 equals 6 cubes")
and trips the coverage only because its walk-back is short too — and that walk-back adds the
**layer**, a real idea a child who got it right has not heard. The exception is that op, by
name, in the pin, with its reason written beside it.

## The authored pile: 14 rules missing their condition

The dominant authored class — and the one this build did **not** try to measure canon-wide,
for reasons below:

- **"the smallest factor"** → *above 1*. Four beats across two lessons: the smallest factor
  of 24 is 1, so the ladder as written never terminates in primes.
- **"Every number above 1 breaks down into primes"** → *Every whole number*.
- **"the smallest one is always partnered with the biggest"** → the smallest factor **above
  1** is partnered with the biggest **below the number**.
- **"the times goes first, then the add. Never left to right."** → *When no parentheses say
  otherwise…*
- **"A pizza cut into 3, and you get 2 of the pieces"** → *cut into 3 **equal** pieces*.
- **"small pieces fit many times, so the answer grows"** → *when the fraction is less than 1*.
- **"18 bottles in 3 hours means 24 bottles in 4 hours"** → *At the same rate, …*
- **"a fact that holds for every triangle ever drawn: 180 take away 110 leaves 70"** → the
  universal fact is the 180, not the 110. Now: *every triangle's three angles total 180*.
- **"a rectangle's area — the long side times the short side"** → *one side times the other*
  (a rectangle can be a square, and this lesson's space contains 4 by 4).
- **"The counts add — they never times."** → *When you add like terms, …*
- **"if your answer came out smaller, you ran the sum forwards by mistake"** → one wrong
  answer does not have one cause. Now: *check whether you took a percent OF the part*.

**"never add" was fixed in BOTH ratio lessons**, though the sweep quoted only one. The same
sentence with the same overreach sits in `pre-u6-filling-in-a-proportion`; fixing one and
leaving the other would leave the canon arguing with itself.

## Why the condition class was NOT measured canon-wide

Every earlier class was closed by measuring it across all ten courses and pinning it at zero.
This one was tried twice — once before this sweep, once after — and **both attempts failed**:

- The textbook rule list (Pythagoras, L'Hôpital, the log laws) found essentially nothing,
  because this canon does not talk that way.
- A list rebuilt from the canon's own wording found 98 — and on sampling, most were false.
  "13 dots shared into groups of 4" needs no "equal"; that is what sharing means. "Roots
  never add" is true. "Every number sits between two tens" is fine in a rounding lesson.
  Narrowing one pattern until it was honest took it from 31 hits to 5.

The four closed classes were all **structural** — an unread number, a word count, a sentence
length — countable without understanding the sentence. Whether a missing condition *matters*
depends on context a scanner cannot see. **This class has to be read.** That is not a gap in
the tooling to be closed later; it is what the sweeps are for, and it raises what the
remaining rounds are worth rather than lowering it.
