An honest read on her progress

Maya is doing well, and she's earned it. She has mastered four of the nine units in Pre-Algebra and is working through decimals now, getting about 91% right on her own — that means she's solving them, not being walked through them.

Her best habit is one I wish more students had: she checks her answer before she commits to it, out loud, without being asked. Her hardest week was the last week of October, when percents genuinely beat her twice. She came back four days in a row until they clicked. That's the part I'd be proudest of.

What she still finds hard is lining up decimal points when the two numbers have a different number of digits. It's a common one and it isn't a worry — but you asked how she's doing, so you should know.

What's hard right now — and the plan

The difficulty: lining up decimal points when the numbers have different lengths (3.5 + 12.75). She knows the rule and forgets it under pressure.

What I'm doing about it: smaller numbers first, then money problems — dollars and cents make the decimal point feel obvious — and I'll keep bringing one back each session until it's ordinary. I'll say so right here when it is.

What helps at home: nothing that feels like school. Let her price out a grocery list.
